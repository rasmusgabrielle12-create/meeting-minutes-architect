# Meeting-type playbook

Select the closest playbook after diagnosing the source. Use it to prioritize evidence, not to create missing sections or facts.

## Product or design review

Emphasize:

- problem and target user
- evaluated options and trade-offs
- approved, rejected, and deferred scope
- acceptance criteria and launch dependencies
- research, design, engineering, analytics, and legal actions

Do not convert positive reactions to a design into approval. Record the decision owner when explicit.

## Project sync or weekly meeting

Emphasize:

- progress since the last checkpoint
- blockers and dependencies
- changes to scope, schedule, or ownership
- commitments before the next checkpoint
- items requiring escalation

Avoid turning status reports into decisions. Combine repeated status updates under the same workstream.

## Customer or sales call

Emphasize:

- customer goals, pain points, constraints, and objections
- commitments made by either party
- requested capabilities, commercial terms, and decision process
- next contact, responsible party, and timing
- uncertainties requiring internal confirmation

Distinguish a customer request from an agreed product commitment. Preserve quotes only when wording materially matters.

## Strategy or executive decision meeting

Emphasize:

- decision question
- options and evaluation criteria
- chosen direction and rationale
- dissent, assumptions, and reversible/irreversible aspects
- milestones, decision owners, and review dates

Keep unresolved assumptions visible. Do not erase dissent to make the minutes appear cleaner.

## Incident review or postmortem

Prefer chronological organization. Emphasize:

- impact and scope
- detection, response, mitigation, and recovery timeline
- contributing factors supported by evidence
- remediation actions, owners, deadlines, and verification
- unanswered technical or process questions

Do not assign blame or root cause without evidence. Distinguish observed cause, contributing condition, and hypothesis.

## Interview or research session

Emphasize:

- research questions
- participant context that affects interpretation
- observed behavior and direct evidence
- synthesized insights and confidence
- follow-up questions

Do not label researcher interpretation as participant fact. Anonymize participants if requested or appropriate.

## Workshop or brainstorming

Emphasize:

- prompt and constraints
- idea clusters
- selection criteria
- shortlisted, rejected, and parked ideas
- experiments or next steps

Idea generation is discussion, not decision. Only selected outcomes with closure signals become decisions.

## Personal retrospective or one-on-one

Emphasize:

- observations and themes
- commitments from each person
- support needed
- follow-up date

Use neutral language for subjective or sensitive statements. Avoid diagnostic labels and unnecessary attribution.

## Generic working session

Use when evidence does not support a more specific meeting type. Organize around agenda topics, decisions, actions, pending items, and risks without adding domain-specific assumptions.
